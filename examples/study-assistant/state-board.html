Study assistant: state board

A chat in the student portal that answers questions about courses, deadlines and regulations from the University's official documents, for about 12,000 students. Read-only: it takes no actions.

  • Surface: chat, drawn at phone width
  • Stakes: medium; high for deadlines and regulations students act on
  • Autonomy: none (answers only)
  • Register: plain English, "you", no slang to confirm
  • Style: neutral template, not the portal's own look
  • Version: 16 November 2026, new design

Coverage

12 of 14 answer states apply and each has a card. 1 more waits on a signal (drawn). 1 does not apply. Plus the sensitive-moment card, which is a proposal until its owners review it.

SSensitive moment

I said something that is not about coursework. Does anyone hear it, and who can actually help me now?

Trigger: a classifier on every incoming message, run before retrieval and separate from the refusal signal. Two levels: risk now (suicidal thoughts, self-harm, a threat, assault, being followed, a medical emergency, for the student or someone else) and distress (not coping, panic, harassment or bullying described, bereavement). It leans toward catching too much. The screen also depends on whether counselling is open now. To build: the classifier and the "counselling open now" signal (clock plus closure days).

Makes a false alarm cost one press instead of a lecture. The question is held, never dropped. Whether to answer straight away at this level is the owner's call.

  • The study task is dropped in A, B and C: no answer, no sources, no example questions.
  • Nothing is done for the student: nobody is notified, no referral is opened. The card says so ("This chat doesn't alert anyone").
  • No thumbs, no regenerate, no "was this useful?" on these messages.
  • No questions back to the student about what happened. The assistant does not take the account.
  • Phone buttons dial on a phone; on a desktop they show the number to copy.
  • Not the refusal style, not the error style, and the tag words carry the state, not the colour.
  • If the classifier misses, "Support & urgent help" is in the header of every screen and opens the same routes.
  • Logged: that it fired, the level, and which route was opened. Never the message text in ordinary analytics.
  • If the answer service is down, this card still shows: it is fixed copy and does not need the model.
  • The conversation goes on normally afterwards if the student moves to something else.

Review with the Head of Counselling (Student Services), the Head of Campus Security and the University's safeguarding lead before shipping. To confirm on this card: the security number (01632 960 100 is an example); 999 as the emergency number; that Campus Security takes calls from students in distress and can attend on campus; how counselling is reached (phone, booking page, drop-in) and its closure days; whether this chat really alerts nobody and who can read it; whether a night support line exists.

Across all states

Interruption

  • Stop keeps the text written so far, marked "Answer interrupted", with Try again. An interrupted answer shows no sources as final.
  • Phone locks or the portal goes to the background: the request carries on and the answer is in the thread on return (to confirm). No push notification.
  • Connection drops mid-answer: same as Stop, plus the offline bar from card 12.

Memory

  • Inside a conversation it keeps the course and level, shown as removable chips above the composer.
  • Between conversations it keeps nothing (assumed). It never says "I'll remember".
  • Whether past conversations are listed for the student, and how long they are stored, is behind the first-screen link. To confirm.

Proactivity

  • Not in this version: the assistant never speaks first and sends no reminders.
  • If deadline reminders are added later, they need the student's opt-in and a different board section.

Cost

  • Nothing is charged to students. Not applicable unless a per-student daily limit exists (to confirm).
  • If one exists: show what is left near the composer when few remain, and a "broke" message with the reset time when it runs out. Card S is never blocked by a limit.

Is chat the right surface?

  • Yes for open questions about regulations. The four or five most frequent deadline questions would be faster as a "Key dates" page in the portal; the chips on the first screen are the bridge.

Small screens and access

  • Every card is drawn at phone width. Sources open as a sheet over the chat; Stop and Send stay above the keyboard.
  • New messages are announced politely to screen readers, once complete; focus moves only for 12 and S.
  • Students who write in another language: to test. If answers are weaker, say which languages it handles on the first screen.
